Introduction to Our Research

A world is emerging in which all kinds of things are connected to the Internet through wireless communications, and the analysis and utilization of data collected from diverse devices improve our quality of life and transform society. A wide variety of wireless communication systems, including mobile communication systems, wireless local area networks (WLANs), and low-power wide-area networks (LPWANs), provide essential information and communication infrastructure for realizing the Internet of Things (IoT) and cyber-physical systems (CPS).

Our laboratory studies distributed wireless networks, in which multiple nodes operate in a distributed and cooperative manner, through theoretical analysis, computer simulations, and experiments using real-world systems, while also leveraging machine learning. These three research approaches are closely interconnected. For example, findings obtained through experiments and computer simulations are incorporated into mathematical models, while design and control methods derived from these models are applied to practical systems. Furthermore, we seek to extend the findings and methods obtained from research on individual communication systems, such as LPWANs, cellular communication systems including local 5G, and WLANs, to other communication systems, with a view toward developing more broadly applicable concepts and design and evaluation methods.

Through these research activities, we aim to contribute to the realization and further development of IoT and CPS.

Theme 1: Low-Power Wide-Area Networks (LPWANs)

Low-power wide-area networks (LPWANs) are wireless networking technologies designed for IoT applications that provide wide-area coverage with low power consumption. We analyze and evaluate radio propagation characteristics and communication quality, focusing primarily on LoRaWAN and Sigfox, which operate in unlicensed frequency bands. Specifically, our research includes predicting the battery life of end devices based on models of their energy consumption mechanisms and improving the reliability and energy efficiency of downlink communications that support device control in CPS.

As IoT use cases, we consider smart cities in densely built-up urban areas and smart agriculture in rural areas. In the area of smart agriculture, we analyzed data collected using an IoT system deployed in a real-world environment through machine learning and developed a yield prediction model for fruit crops grown in controlled environments. Furthermore, toward integrated sensing and communication (ISAC), which has recently attracted considerable attention, we are exploring the potential of sensing using low-power wide-area wireless signals.

Keywords : Low-Power Wide-Area Wireless Signals, Real-World IoT, Energy Consumption Modeling, Downlink Control, Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC)

Theme 2: Distributed Machine Learning over Wireless Networks

Distributed machine learning enables multiple devices and computers to collaboratively train machine learning models. Our research focuses on wireless federated learning, in which multiple wireless devices participate in the learning process. We are developing a framework for comparing and evaluating wireless federated learning and conventional centralized learning. We are also investigating client selection methods based on wireless communication quality. In addition, from the perspective of sharing learning tasks between devices and a server, we are exploring wireless split learning. In this approach, a deep neural network is partitioned between the device side and the server side for collaborative training.

We have also developed a prototype edge AI system designed to operate on IoT devices. The system performs recognition and inference on the device using deep metric learning and transmits only the resulting information, thereby reducing the amount of communication data. Toward its application to smart agriculture, we plan to conduct a detailed performance evaluation of the proposed system and further improve its design.

Keywords : Wireless Communication Environments, Deep Learning, Federated Learning, Split Learning, Metric Learning

Theme 3: Private 5G

Local 5G is a private 5G network that companies, universities, and other organizations can deploy and operate on their own premises. Our research focuses on a local 5G system deployed in a structurally complex underground indoor area at the Shinagawa Campus of Tokai University. The system consists of multiple radio units (RUs) distributed throughout the area. Each cell is assigned a different physical cell identity (PCI), while all cells operate on the same frequency. We focus particularly on downlink inter-cell co-channel interference in this environment. By measuring and analyzing radio propagation characteristics and communication quality, we aim to establish an optimal coverage design method that takes such interference into account.

We also focus on 5G fixed wireless access (5G FWA), in which local 5G is used as a backhaul connection and Wi-Fi devices are connected through customer premises equipment (CPE). We evaluate the effects of the additional Wi-Fi connection on quality of service (QoS) and quality of experience (QoE) by comparing this configuration with direct device connections to local 5G. We also plan to investigate techniques for improving QoS and QoE in 5G FWA environments.

Keywords : Private 5G, Distributed RUs, Inter-Cell Co-Channel Interference, 5G Fixed Wireless Access (5G FWA), QoS/QoE

Theme 4: Wireless Local Area Networks (WLANs)

Wireless local area networks (WLANs), commonly known as Wi-Fi, primarily operate in unlicensed frequency bands. Along with cellular communication systems, WLANs are widely used in homes, universities, companies, public facilities, and many other locations. We develop mathematical models of the Distributed Coordination Function (DCF), an autonomous distributed medium access control mechanism adopted in IEEE 802.11. Using theoretical analysis and computer simulations, we evaluate performance metrics such as throughput, delay, and frame collisions, with the aim of developing new performance improvement methods. As one example, we proposed a frame aggregation method that transmits multiple data frames together and demonstrated that it can significantly improve throughput compared with conventional methods.

We have also studied multi-hop wireless communications and device-to-device networks, in which multiple wireless nodes relay data among one another, as commonly seen in mesh Wi-Fi networks. Through these studies, we have clarified how route establishment methods and network configurations affect wireless energy efficiency [bits/J] and have proposed methods for improving it.

Keywords : Autonomous Distributed Wireless Access Control, Theoretical Performance Analysis, Throughput, Wireless Energy Efficiency [bits/J], Device-to-Device Communications
